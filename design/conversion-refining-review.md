## Correct building functions

| Building | Function in the owner's concept | Deck evidence |

The owner's latest instruction places conversion in a separate building and
refining/recycling in the building now under discussion. Earlier deck notes
allow different siting arrangements; the latest instruction governs this draft.
The refiner diagram represents interconnected liquid-processing equipment.
It does not establish a sequence of seven solid-object workstations or an
EBR-II-sized annular process cell. The converter concept still involves incoming
solid material and outgoing material handling; describing the whole system as
liquid-only would also be inaccurate.

## What the deck establishes about space

Slide 22 states a proposed converter reaction chamber of 2 m length and 1 m
diameter. This is a component dimension, not the complete machine envelope or
a facility size. Slide 21 illustrates attached handling equipment but provides
no overall dimensions. Slide 19 has no dimensional scale for the refiner.
The deck does not supply a complete equipment schedule, receiving/maintenance
layout, shielding design or building area schedule for either facility.
Its process-capacity and performance statements remain author proposals; this
review does not verify them or derive processing equipment sizes from them.

Consequently, no defensible replacement building length, width or height is
established here. The former 15 × 12 × 5 m rectangle and D-044's 28 m / 8 m
horseshoe are visual studies, not evidence-based facility estimates. D-044's
seven equipment bays, 1.5 m shielding bands and 12 × 12 m coolant annex must
not become inherited requirements.

## Architectural information needed next

Use current reports to identify already specified equipment envelopes and
facility provisions. Record each source and revision, distinguishing machine
footprints from maintenance/removal space and complete building dimensions.
A facility-level space schedule should separately identify the supplied process
area, personnel/observation areas, receiving and dispatch, maintenance space,
utilities and other support rooms. Process-equipment sizing, shielding and
containment design remain specialist engineering inputs.

The horseshoe remains the owner's preferred observation/access concept, but
its size and number of equipment locations are open. EBR-II remains a reference
for remote observation and handling architecture only. The current Blender
scene is unchanged pending a substantiated layout basis.

### Findings relevant to the building scope

| Source location | Reported scope | Consequence for the architectural record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Converter, Introduction and Figure 1 | Conversion of incoming spent fuel precedes refining | Keep conversion and refining as separate building functions |
| Converter, Introduction and Overview | Solid-material preparation/handling accompanies the converter, which supplies crude salt | A chamber envelope alone cannot represent the complete converter facility |
| Refiner, Introduction | Receives crude converter salt and used WISTR fuel; starts and ends with molten salt | The refining building has two feed interfaces and a fuel-return interface to WISTR |
| Refiner, Introduction and alternative concept sections | Several arrangements remain under discussion | Do not depict every alternative as equipment simultaneously installed |
| Both documents | Process concepts and supporting discussion, without a complete dimensional facility schedule | No new building length, width or height can be attributed to these reports |

The reports support liquid transfer in the refiner, rather than a solid-fuel
fabrication/assembly line. They do not establish a particular number of bays,
a smaller building, remote-maintenance clearances or qualified shielding.
The presentation's 2 m × 1 m converter chamber remains a presentation-sourced
component allowance; it was not found as an overall equipment dimension in
these reports. No equipment scale is inferred from the schematic drawings.

Treat proposed output purity and waste classifications as source claims, not
as established grounds for omitting controlled material-handling areas. This
review neither validates separation performance nor specifies processing
conditions or equipment capacity.
