# Fast Salt System — workshop talk outline

Draft for discussion, 10 October 2026. **10 main slides; 14 minutes of planned speaking, 1 minute of margin, then 5 minutes for questions.** Workshop dates: **October 27–29, 2026**, confirmed by the project owner. The specific presentation day/time has not yet been recorded.

Working title: **Fast Salt System - Progress**

## Central message

The Fast Salt System is being developed to use fuel derived from spent nuclear fuel in WISTR. Current work gives the reactor concept a more explicit mechanical layout and remote maintenance sequence, while university research and steam-generator development inform the next steps toward an integrated plant concept.

The audience already has some familiarity. Use a short system recap and spend the talk on the current design, the research supporting it, and the work needed to connect components. Present modeled features as design concepts; attribute analysis results to their specific studies.

### 2. The system and its purpose

**On screen:** SNF conversion → fuel preparation and reuse → WISTR → heat transport and electricity generation. Show the fuel-return connection and separate waste outputs at a functional level.

**Speaking points:** Establish where WISTR fits in the system. Briefly distinguish the salt fuel from the separate circulating coolant. Keep converter and refiner discussion at the level needed to understand the overall project; detailed processing and economics belong outside this short progress talk.

**Source:** Supplied revision 5 deck, slides 3–4 and 26; [system overview](../design/system-overview.md). Describe the flow as the proposed system, not an operating installation.

### 3. Research supporting WISTR

**On screen:** UM work on core design and fuel-cycle behavior; thermal-fluid studies; accident-scenario work in progress. One research result or figure should carry the main explanation.

**Speaking points:** Give the university research proper credit before showing the new mechanical model. Select one result that explains a current design choice. Explain which configuration was analyzed. Later accident work is ongoing according to the owner; add a specific finding only when its supporting material is available.

**Source:** [Study register](../research/studies.md), especially RS-004 to RS-006, and [UM overview W10](../publications/index.md#w10). A figure and page number still need selection from the original research source. This outline does not select or restate a quantitative result from that report.

**Fallback:** If no new figure is supplied, show a concise record of the documented study areas with citations and explain the current research questions. Do not label historical results as new workshop progress.

### 4. The six-sector reactor

**On screen:** Six sectors; core diameter 3.8 m; active height 3.4 m; separate coolant tubes and fuel-containing sectors. Label the radial absorber locations.

**Speaking points:** Orient the audience in a plan view, then relate it to the cutaway. Explain why the model includes reflectors, separators, service tubes, and removable caps. The sector arrangement sets up the later maintenance sequence.

**Source:** Revision 5 slides 6 and 8–11; [current design basis](../design/design-basis.md); [reactor guide](../../fastSaltModels/docs/reactor-study.md).

**Visual preparation:** Use the [refreshed handling-and-plug baseline views](visual-assets.md). Use an operating pose for the basic layout; reserve the raised-sector pose for maintenance. Keep detailed dimensions in backup.

### 5. Coolant and vessel air paths

**On screen:** Two clearly distinguished paths: salt through downcomer, bottom plenum, core tubes, and hot plenum; air down outside the baffle and back up around the vessel into the hot hood.

**Speaking points:** Trace each path once. Identify the three coolant legs connecting the reactor and steam generators. Show the nested hood arrangement and short duct connections as the current concept. Describe natural-draft air cooling as the intended function; this mechanical view does not establish its performance.

**Source:** [Reactor guide](../../fastSaltModels/docs/reactor-study.md) and the owner-defined geometry. Avoid placing both circuits in one unexplained color scheme. Do not use the 1.4 m modeled pipe diameter as a completed flow-sizing result.

### 6. Remote sector maintenance

**On screen:** Three stages: cap removed; sector raised and retained; top reflector and core removed separately.

**Speaking points:** Explain that service connections are disconnected remotely and the core tube plug stays with the core. The four-bar arrangement raises the sector toward the wall. The retained peripheral reflector provides the maintenance position while the top reflector and core can be lifted separately. Show the common lifting-tool concept briefly; keep latch and plug details for questions.

**Source:** [Reactor guide](../../fastSaltModels/docs/reactor-study.md) and [preserved handling snapshot](../../fastSaltModels/baselines/wistr-reactor-handling-2026-10-08/).

**Visual preparation:** Use three stills with matching orientation. An optional 10–15 second clip can explain lifting-bar engagement, but the existing lifting-head animation is not a complete maintenance-cycle demonstration. Retain a still-image fallback. These are conceptual poses, not an operating procedure or a qualified handling system.

### 7. Steam-generator development

**On screen:** MSU student design study; preliminary thermal estimate; equipment envelope and connection definition to develop.

**Speaking points:** Explain the steam generator's place between the salt coolant and steam power system. Credit the students and describe what their work will contribute. The preliminary calculation uses three generators at 500 MW each for the 1500 MW thermal target; identify this as the worksheet's equal-duty case, not a verified operating capability. Keep the software tools and detailed equations in backup.

**Source:** Revision 5 slides 15–17; [MSU brief](../collaboration/steam-generator-project.md); [Hurmet conversion and checks](../calculations/steam-generator/conversion-check.md); [IF-001](../design/interfaces.md#if-001-reactor-and-steam-generator).

**Visual preparation:** Use an existing concept image or a student-provided view, labeled by source. Do not represent the current illustrative casing as the students' completed design.

### 8. A provisional plant installation

**On screen:** Reactor building, steam generators, remote handling/storage, and connection to the steam power plant.

**Speaking points:** Show how the component work fits spatially. Highlight maintenance access and the routes leaving the reactor building. Explain what is established in the concept and what is still represented by equipment envelopes.

**Source:** [Installation guide](../../fastSaltModels/docs/installation.md); revision 5 slide 14 for the earlier plant context.

**Visual preparation:** The [working installation views](visual-assets.md) now include the detailed reactor and three coolant pairs; central hood ducts remain short stubs for later routing. The existing building cutaway does not show a developed turbine/condenser plant; create a clearly provisional site arrangement for that context if time permits. Avoid new detailed plant engineering solely to fill a slide.

**On screen:** Relate current geometry to analyzed revisions; complete component interfaces; develop the installation and remote maintenance concept; incorporate ongoing research results.
